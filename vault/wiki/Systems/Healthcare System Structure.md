# Healthcare System Structure

## Summary

Healthcare systems can be compared by examining who is covered, who finances care, what benefits are included, how delivery is organized, how providers are paid, and how cost, equity, quality, and outcomes are managed. The United States distributes these decisions across government, employers, health plans, providers, product companies, and intermediaries, which makes responsibility for the patient experience difficult to locate.

## September 2 Sponsors of Care and Coverage

The money-stack module asks who finances care, who carries risk, and who decides. Insurance protects assets, pools risk, operationalizes budgets, and supports provider payment. The US employment-based system emerged partly from wartime wage controls and tax policy. Employers and government remain central sponsors, but both increasingly seek to limit exposure, often by transferring cost or responsibility to households, plans, providers, or specialized vendors. Coverage design reflects deeper beliefs about whether healthcare is a right, privilege, or commodity.

## August 31 Health-system structure, costs, and comparison

This session supplies the comparison framework: government role, coverage and financing, benefits, delivery organization, payment, cost containment, affordability, equity, technology, quality, and outcomes. The US system appears complicated, fragmented, expensive, inequitable, innovative, and oriented more toward medical care than health. International benchmarking helps reveal the consequences of structural choices without pretending that another country's model can be copied intact.

## Why so many middlemen exist

US healthcare combines complicated products, fragmented financing, specialized regulation, and opaque prices. Intermediaries emerge to connect parties, process transactions, negotiate terms, aggregate purchasing power, distribute products, manage carved-out benefits, or accept risks that sponsors and providers do not want to manage alone. Examples include health plans, brokers, consultants, third-party administrators, group purchasing organizations, wholesalers, pharmacy benefit managers, and technology-enabled distribution platforms. [Class 9/9/26] Intermediaries can create real value by reducing search and transaction costs, building specialized expertise, and coordinating complex flows. They can also add fees, obscure money flows, exploit information advantages, and become entrenched. The useful question is not whether an intermediary exists, but whether its contribution exceeds its cost and whether its incentives align with patients and sponsors.

## The course arc in one sentence

Healthcare outcomes emerge from the interaction of system goals, financing sources, risk allocation, market structure, intermediaries, payment rules, organizational capabilities, and patient circumstances.
