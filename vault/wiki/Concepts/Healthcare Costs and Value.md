# Healthcare Costs and Value

## Summary

The United States spends more on healthcare than peer countries without consistently producing better access or outcomes. The course materials connect this pattern to high prices, administrative costs, fragmented financing, uneven access, and tradeoffs among cost, quality, equity, choice, and innovation. Value therefore depends on whose outcomes and spending are being measured.

## Role of Government

Government finances programs, regulates markets, and shapes coverage and payment, but it operates alongside employers, households, plans, and providers. This mixed structure creates multiple eligibility rules, benefit designs, networks, and payment systems, making accountability diffuse and increasing administrative work.

## Cost and Spending

The study guide emphasizes that high provider prices and administrative costs explain more of the US spending gap than unusually high utilization alone. The course deck reports $5.7 trillion in US health spending in 2025 and projects average annual growth of about 5.4% through 2034, when spending could reach 20.6% of GDP. These are course-provided projections, not independently updated figures.

## Coverage and Access

Coverage does not by itself produce access. Networks, deductibles, formularies, appointment capacity, reimbursement, geography, and affordability determine whether a covered person can actually receive an appropriate service.

## Innovation and Technology

Scientific and technical capability can advance quickly while delivery, payment, regulation, and workflow change slowly. A useful technology still needs evidence, coverage, reimbursement, organizational adoption, and patient affordability before it can produce broad benefit.

## Limits of These Sources

The deck mixes historical comparisons with projections, and the study guide notes that some supporting quantitative evidence comes from data through 2011. The sources explain course concepts but do not establish that any single reform will lower total cost or improve outcomes.
